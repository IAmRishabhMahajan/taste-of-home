The flow should be:

💌 Physical invitation → QR code → 🌐 A Taste of Home website → RSVP + dietary requirements + dish allocation

And importantly, guests shouldn't freely choose any dish they want without seeing what's already been claimed.

1. The website should have 4 stages
① RSVP

Are you joining us?

Yes / No
Name
Phone/email
Number attending
② Tell us about your food

Instead of:

“What are you bringing?”

Give them categories first.

For example:

CategoryTarget
🍗 Main protein4–5
🥬 Vegetarian main4
🥔 Potato/vegetable sides5
🥗 Salads4
🍚 Rice/grains3
🍝 Pasta/noodle dishes3
🥖 Bread/rolls3
🧀 Appetisers/snacks5
🥧 Desserts5
🍪 Childhood/nostalgic treats4
🥤 Drinks5

These don't need to be exact — they're targets.

Then the website can say:

Pick a category you'd like to contribute to. We'll make sure the table has a good balance!

2. Make the choices specific

This is the important bit.

Someone selects:

🥖 Bread & Baked Goods

Choose something that can be shared by 8–10 people.

Someone else:

🥗 Salad / Vegetable

We're looking for something fresh or vegetable-forward.

Someone else:

🍗 Protein

Chicken, turkey, fish or another meat you enjoy — please note ingredients.

Someone vegetarian:

🌱 Vegetarian Main

A substantial vegetarian dish that can serve 8–10.

This means you aren't relying on everyone to interpret “bring a dish” differently.

3. HAVE A LIVE "WHAT'S NEEDED" SECTION

This is where your website could be really cool.

After someone RSVPs, show:

What does our table need?

🟢 Still needed

2 × salads
2 × vegetables
2 × bread
1 × vegetarian main
2 × desserts
2 × drinks

🔴 Already covered

Rice
Curry
Pasta
Chicken

Then people select from what's actually needed.

That solves your “everyone brings biryani” problem. 😂

4. Dietary requirements need to be separate

Don't make dietary information dependent on what they're bringing.

Have:

About your food

I eat:

☐ Everything

☐ Vegetarian

☐ Vegan

☐ Halal

☐ No beef

☐ No pork

☐ No seafood

☐ Gluten-free

☐ Other

Allergies

Free text box.

Then you have one master dietary matrix.

For example:

GuestBringingDietary
SarahSaladVegetarian
JinDumplingsNo pork
PriyaCurryVegetarian
AlexBreadGluten-free

This becomes incredibly useful when you're planning the table.

5. BUT I would add one more question

This is what makes A Taste of Home different.

🏠 Tell us about your dish

What are you bringing?

Dish name

Where does it come from?

“My mum makes this every Christmas...”

What does it remind you of?

That information can then become your food labels at the actual event.

6. The physical invitation should NOT contain all the logistics

Keep it beautiful.

The physical card should essentially say:

A Taste of Home
Friendsgiving 2026

From our homes to one table.

We're bringing together our favourite people for an evening of food, stories, friendship and gratitude.

Bring something that tastes like home to you — and come meet the people who make this year worth being grateful for.

[DATE]

[TIME]

NCPV MCR

Your seat is waiting.

RSVP → [QR CODE]

Then underneath, tiny:

Please RSVP by [DATE] and tell us what you're bringing.

That's it.

7. The QR code is the transition

When they scan it, the website opens with:

Welcome to A Taste of Home 🤎

Before we gather around one table, we need to know what everyone's bringing to it.

[ RSVP ]

Then the website takes them through the whole experience.

And I would make the website visually feel like the invitation

Cream background + kraft textures + burgundy/olive accents + handwritten typography + little food illustrations.

So the physical invitation feels like a preview of the website, rather than two unrelated things.

The key is that you maintain the master list while guests only see the available categories/options. That gives you control over the balance of food and dietary coverage without making the RSVP feel like an administrative Google Form.
